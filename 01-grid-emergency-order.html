Federal emergency order gives PJM extra tools as October heat strains the grid serving northern Illinois

DOE Order 202-26-50, in effect Oct. 13–16, lets PJM run listed plants past their limits and, as a last resort, move data centers onto backup power before cutting service to homes.

Northern Illinois · October 13, 2026 · 3 min read

The U.S. Department of Energy has issued an emergency order giving PJM Interconnection, the grid operator for northern Illinois, extra authority to keep the lights on through Friday as unusually warm fall weather collides with a heavy season of planned power plant maintenance.

Order No. 202-26-50, signed by Energy Secretary Chris Wright on Oct. 12 and effective Oct. 13 through Oct. 16, 2026, does two main things. It allows PJM to dispatch a list of specified generating units beyond their normal operating limits, including environmental permit restrictions, when needed for reliability. And it authorizes PJM to direct backup generation at data centers and other large electricity users to run as a last resort before PJM would interrupt firm customer load, the step commonly known as rolling blackouts.

PJM serves all or parts of 13 states and the District of Columbia, including ComEd's territory in northern Illinois.

How tight is the grid?

In its Oct. 11 request to DOE, PJM said it was forecast to be within 900 megawatts of its reserve margin on Oct. 14. It forecast demand at or around 111,500 MW on Oct. 13 and said generation outages were anticipated to reach roughly 61,591 MW. PJM said the most acute problems were in the BGE, PEPCO and Dominion zones in the Mid-Atlantic.

In an operations update Tuesday, PJM said generation outages were about 61,000 MW that day and that, as of 2 p.m., forecast demand was 112,447 MW for Tuesday and 113,312 MW for Wednesday, numbers it said were subject to change.

PJM extended a Maximum Generation Alert and Load Management Alert through Oct. 14 and is recalling all active generation maintenance outages effective noon Wednesday, Oct. 14. On Tuesday it also issued a pre-emergency load management action, calling on demand response customers who are paid to cut use, to help cover the afternoon peak and the evening hours after solar output drops to zero.

“Unseasonable weather patterns are providing fewer days to perform important maintenance or construction on the generation and transmission equipment serving our footprint,” said Mike Bryson, PJM's senior vice president of system operations. “This is the new reality that grid operators are facing.”

No blackouts ordered

PJM says the alert itself requires no action from customers, and it has not had to use large-load backup generation under this order to date. No firm load shedding has been ordered. The order is designed to reduce the chance that it will be.

Key facts
  • Order: DOE 202-26-50, effective Oct. 13–16, 2026
  • Reserve margin: forecast within 900 MW on Oct. 14
  • Generation outages: ~61,000 MW
  • Max Generation Alert extended through Oct. 14; maintenance outages recalled noon Oct. 14
  • No load shed and no large-load backup generation used to date

Why it matters for your home

The order exists to protect homes from forced outages when supply runs thin. It also shows that grid operators now plan for stress in seasons once considered easy. A home battery charged by rooftop solar can store energy you control. Depending on system size and setup, it can power chosen circuits if the grid goes down. Solar panels without a battery normally shut off during an outage for lineworker safety. ComEd offers distributed generation rebates for solar and battery storage. Ask any installer for a written estimate of what your system would actually back up.
