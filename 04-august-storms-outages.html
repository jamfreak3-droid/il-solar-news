Outages

Two months after storms cut power to nearly 410,000 ComEd customers, the south suburbs remember the dark

The Aug. 11–12 storms, including tornadoes, hit Homewood, Flossmoor, Lansing, Chicago Heights, Tinley Park and Joliet hardest. Across the state line, some NIPSCO customers waited nearly two weeks.

Northern Illinois · October 13, 2026 · 2 min read

When severe storms tore across northern Illinois Aug. 11–12, they caused nearly 410,000 ComEd customer outages, one of the utility's largest restoration efforts of the year.

ComEd said on Aug. 15 that it had restored power to about 99% of affected customers. More than 4,500 employees, contractors and mutual-assistance workers supported the response after destructive winds, tornadoes and repeated rounds of severe weather damaged electric equipment.

“This week's storms were not an isolated event, but part of a historic summer of severe weather across northern Illinois,” said David Perez, ComEd's executive vice president and chief operating officer.

Southland hit hardest

As of 4 p.m. Aug. 13, about 52,000 homes and businesses were still without power, CBS News Chicago reported, mainly in Homewood, Flossmoor, Lansing, Chicago Heights, Tinley Park and Joliet. The National Weather Service confirmed six tornadoes in the south and southwest suburbs. One was an EF-1 that hit the Homewood, Glenwood and Thornton area, damaging a strip mall, apartment buildings and several homes.

“Those pockets, we are having to rebuild the grid. It's not a repair at that point,” said Jeremy Smith, ComEd's vice president of distribution and operations, citing new poles, new lines and rain-soaked work sites.

The human toll showed at Homewood-Flossmoor High School, where staff handed out meals. “We have not had power since the storm,” resident Tawana Jordan told CBS. “Last night, it was just so hot in our house.” Homewood School District 154 pushed the start of its school year back a week.

Nearly two weeks in NW Indiana

Across the border, NIPSCO customers fared worse. On Aug. 22, ABC7 Chicago reported that thousands of northwest Indiana residents had been without power since Aug. 11, nearly two weeks. More than 30,000 NIPSCO customers were still out that afternoon, with Gary and Portage among the hardest-hit communities. ComEd sent more than 60 employees to help.

Residents described ruined furniture from moisture, spoiled food and costly hotel stays. A class-action lawsuit accusing NIPSCO of inadequate vegetation management has been filed. NIPSCO denies the allegations.

Why it matters for your home

Most families got power back within days. For those who didn't, the cost was spoiled food, hot homes and nights in hotels. Solar panels by themselves usually shut down when the grid goes out. Paired with a battery and the right equipment, they can keep essentials like the refrigerator, sump pump, Wi-Fi and some lights running, and recharge from the sun each day. How long a battery lasts depends on its size and what you run, so ask for a backup plan matched to your home's priority loads.
