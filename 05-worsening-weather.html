ComEd: 2026 has brought more storms than all of 2025, and it isn't over

A July 27 storm knocked out power to about 530,000 customers. By mid-August, ComEd was counting its 20th major weather event of the year.

Northern Illinois · October 13, 2026 · 2 min read

Northern Illinois is having its roughest storm year in recent memory, according to ComEd, and the numbers back that up.

July: a half-million in the dark

Storms on Monday, July 27, produced wind gusts above 80 mph, knocking over power poles and damaging trees and buildings across the Chicago area, NBC Chicago reported. ComEd said outages reached more than half a million customers at the height of the storms.

“In total, approximately 530,000 customers lost power from the extreme wave of weather systems impacting our region,” ComEd Chief Operating Officer David Perez said, according to CBS News Chicago. He called it “a massive weather event” requiring a multi-day restoration. More than 2,300 workers were in the field the next morning, 1,200 of them from outside the area.

The National Weather Service confirmed four tornadoes from that day's storms, CBS reported. They included an EF-1 with peak winds near 110 mph that touched down in or near Lansing, along with EF-0 tornadoes near Glen Ellyn and in Orland Park. In Hazel Crest, winds tore walls off townhomes and displaced dozens of people. The Red Cross opened shelters in Steger and Gurnee.

In a July 28 notice to customers, ComEd wrote: “The reality is that northern Illinois is experiencing a level of severe weather unlike what we've seen in recent years. In fact, our service area has already experienced more storms this year than in all of 2025, with five months remaining in the year.”

August: number 20

Barely two weeks later, the Aug. 11–12 storms caused nearly 410,000 more customer outages. CBS News Chicago reported that ComEd called that event its 20th major weather event of the year and described 2026's weather as unprecedented in the last two decades.

Perez said the August storms were “part of a historic summer of severe weather across northern Illinois” and underscored “the importance of building an electric system capable of withstanding increasingly frequent extreme weather.”

Weather is now pressuring the grid from another direction too. This week, unusually warm October temperatures pushed PJM, the regional grid operator, to seek a federal emergency order. PJM said there were fewer mild days to finish needed maintenance.

Key facts
  • July 27, 2026: 80+ mph gusts; ~530,000 ComEd customers lost power (COO David Perez)
  • By July 28, ComEd said 2026 had more storms than all of 2025
  • Aug. 11–12 storms: ComEd's 20th major weather event of 2026; ~410,000 outages

Why it matters for your home

Utilities are rebuilding for tougher weather, but restoration still takes time after big storms, sometimes days. A home battery paired with solar is one way to keep critical loads running while crews work. A portable generator is another, and officials stress it must always run outdoors in a well-ventilated area because of carbon monoxide risk. A solar-charged battery runs quietly, needs no fuel and recharges from the sun. Size it to the loads that matter most to your family.
