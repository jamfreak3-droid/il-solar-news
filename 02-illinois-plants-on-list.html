Five Illinois gas plants are on the emergency list to run past their limits

Units in Manhattan, Crete, Morris, Cordova and Elgin appear on PJM's list of resources that may exceed emissions limits under this week's federal order.

Northern Illinois · October 13, 2026 · 2 min read

Several natural gas power plants in Illinois are among the generators PJM may run beyond their normal emissions limits under this week's federal emergency order, according to the list PJM filed with the Department of Energy.

Exhibit A to PJM's request, Version 1, submitted Oct. 12, 2026, lists the “Specified Resources” covered by DOE Order No. 202-26-50. PJM may dispatch these units past their operating limits only as needed to maintain reliability while the order is in effect, Oct. 13 through Oct. 16.

The Illinois units

Lincoln, Manhattan: eight combustion turbines of about 77 MW each. The listed constraint is Illinois CEJA 12-month rolling environmental limits on nitrogen oxides (NOx), carbon monoxide (CO), particulate matter (PM), volatile organic material (VOM) and carbon dioxide (CO2).

Crete: one combustion turbine, Crete 4, about 75.2 MW, with the same CEJA 12-month rolling limits on NOx, CO, PM, VOM and CO2.

Three Rivers, Morris: two combined-cycle units of about 609 MW each, listed with a NOx rolling-hour-average constraint.

Cordova: one combined-cycle unit of about 500 MW, listed with 12-month emissions limits for NOx, CO2 and sulfur dioxide (SO2).

Elgin: four combustion turbines of about 120–121 MW each, also listed with 12-month limits for NOx, CO2 and SO2.

CEJA is Illinois' Climate and Equitable Jobs Act, the state's landmark energy law. In its application, PJM asked that emissions, run hours and fuel burned under the order not count toward 12-month or 30-day rolling-average limits. The DOE order grants that, citing the Federal Power Act. The order also requires PJM to report daily to DOE which units actually ran under the allowance.

A bottleneck at the state line

PJM also pointed to transmission limits. Among them, west-to-east power transfers are being limited by loading on the Crete–St. Johns 345-kilovolt tie line with MISO, the grid operator for central and southern Illinois. PJM noted that MISO issued its own Capacity Advisory and Conservative Operations starting Oct. 12, which could limit how much help is available across that seam.

Being on the list does not mean a plant has exceeded its limits. It means PJM has permission to run it harder if conditions require.

Key facts
  • Illinois units listed: Lincoln (Manhattan), Crete 4, Three Rivers (Morris), Cordova, Elgin
  • CEJA 12-month rolling limits (NOx, CO, PM, VOM, CO2) cited for Lincoln and Crete
  • Crete–St. Johns 345 kV tie with MISO limiting west-to-east transfers
  • MISO Capacity Advisory from Oct. 12

Why it matters for your home

When the grid is tight, the backstop is often older, fast-start gas turbines near our neighborhoods, sometimes running beyond their usual pollution limits. Rooftop solar adds daytime supply right where it is used. A home battery can store that energy for the evening, when PJM says solar output drops to zero and stress peaks. For a household, the practical benefit is having some power you control on hard days, and less dependence on how the regional system performs.
