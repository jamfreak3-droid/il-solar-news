Power, prices and storms: what northern Illinois homeowners should know

Six short reports on this week's grid emergency, rising ComEd prices and a record storm year, with sources.

Updated October 13, 2026
Grid Watch

Federal emergency order gives PJM extra tools as October heat strains the grid serving northern Illinois

DOE Order 202-26-50, in effect Oct. 13–16, lets PJM run listed plants past their limits and, as a last resort, move data centers onto backup power before cutting service to homes.

Local Impact

Five Illinois gas plants are on the emergency list to run past their limits

Units in Manhattan, Crete, Morris, Cordova and Elgin appear on PJM's list of resources that may exceed emissions limits under this week's federal order.

Your Bill

Data centers are driving record grid capacity prices, and ComEd customers are paying

CUB says the 2026–27 capacity price is about 11 times what it was two years earlier, and that ComEd prices should stay elevated at least through May 2028.

Outages

Two months after storms cut power to nearly 410,000 ComEd customers, the south suburbs remember the dark

The Aug. 11–12 storms, including tornadoes, hit Homewood, Flossmoor, Lansing, Chicago Heights, Tinley Park and Joliet hardest. Across the state line, some NIPSCO customers waited nearly two weeks.

Weather

ComEd: 2026 has brought more storms than all of 2025, and it isn't over

A July 27 storm knocked out power to about 530,000 customers. By mid-August, ComEd was counting its 20th major weather event of the year.

Rates

ComEd's new fall price is a bit lower than summer's, but still about 53% above 2024

The price to compare from October 2026 through May 2027 is 10.103 cents per kWh. CUB warns prices remain elevated because of data center demand.
