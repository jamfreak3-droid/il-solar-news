Rates

ComEd's new fall price is a bit lower than summer's, but still about 53% above 2024

The price to compare from October 2026 through May 2027 is 10.103 cents per kWh. CUB warns prices remain elevated because of data center demand.

Northern Illinois · October 13, 2026 · 2 min read

ComEd customers got a small break this month, though prices remain far above where they were two years ago.

ComEd's new “price to compare” for October 2026 through May 2027 is 10.103 cents per kilowatt-hour, the Citizens Utility Board reported Sept. 28. The figure includes the supply price and a transmission charge. It is nearly 3% below this past summer's price of 10.399 cents but about 53% higher than two years ago.

The price to compare is the rate customers should weigh against any offer from an alternative electricity supplier.

Summer bills went up

In a May 14 announcement, ComEd said the average residential bill would rise $2 to $3 a month starting June 1, 2026, because of the PJM capacity auction. The utility said capacity charges passed on to customers have risen by $12 a month for the average residential customer since 2022. It said they would have risen closer to $16 without a state credit under Illinois' Climate and Equitable Jobs Act.

ComEd stressed that these supply charges are not ComEd costs. It does not own power plants and does not profit from PJM auction prices. CUB agrees that ComEd passes supply costs through with no markup. CUB adds that ComEd does profit from delivery rate increases, including a $243 million hike last year.

A credit that's shrinking

A CEJA credit on bills, the Carbon Free Energy Resource Adjustment, has softened the blow. CUB says it ranged from about 1.6 cents per kWh in September to about 0.115 cents in October. The credit is set to end in June 2027.

CUB's advice

CUB urges customers to be wary of alternative supplier pitches. It says Illinois consumers have lost about $2.3 billion to alternative suppliers since 2015. CUB also recommends weatherization and other efficiency steps, and contacting ComEd about payment plans and assistance. LIHEAP applications open to all income-eligible households Nov. 1. CUB also points customers toward community solar and rooftop solar resources.

ComEd, for its part, highlights distributed generation rebates. It says a typical 7 kW residential solar system can earn up to $300 per kW, roughly $2,100, with battery incentives of up to $300 per kWh, averaging about $4,050 for a typical system.

Key facts
  • Price to compare Oct 2026–May 2027: 10.103¢/kWh (supply + transmission)
  • Summer 2026: 10.399¢/kWh; new price ~3% lower
  • ~53% above two years ago
  • ComEd: average residential bill +$2–3/month from June 1, 2026 due to PJM capacity

Why it matters for your home

The rate you pay for grid power can change twice a year, and CUB expects it to stay elevated for years. Solar shifts part of your supply to energy you produce yourself, and a battery lets you use it when you need it, including during outages. ComEd's own rebates for solar and batteries can lower upfront cost. CUB notes the CEJA adjustment works in reverse for net-metering customers. Ask for a quote that spells out rebates, net metering and your expected bill using your real usage.
