Data centers are driving record grid capacity prices, and ComEd customers are paying

CUB says the 2026–27 capacity price is about 11 times what it was two years earlier, and that ComEd prices should stay elevated at least through May 2028.

Northern Illinois · October 13, 2026 · 2 min read

The cost of reserve power on the grid serving northern Illinois has hit record levels, and the Citizens Utility Board says the main driver is electricity demand from new and proposed data centers.

PJM Interconnection's capacity auction for June 2026 through May 2027 set a price of $329.17 per megawatt-day. According to CUB, that is about 22% higher than the previous year's price for ComEd territory and about 11 times the price two years earlier. CUB lists the 2024–25 price at $28.92 per MW-day.

Capacity payments go to power plant owners for committing to have electricity available if demand spikes, such as on a hot afternoon when everyone runs the air conditioning. CUB says capacity typically makes up roughly 20% of ComEd's supply price.

What's behind it

“The Independent Market Monitor for PJM said the primary reason for the spike in prices was data center-related energy demand,” CUB wrote in a June 10 explainer. CUB also faults PJM policies, including a compressed auction schedule that, it says, gives generators too little time to respond by building new plants.

Later auctions have stayed high. CUB lists $333.44 per MW-day for 2027–28 and $325 for 2028–29. CUB says ComEd's price is expected to stay elevated at least through May 2028.

ComEd's current price is about 53% higher than two years ago, CUB says. ComEd does not mark up supply costs. Under state law it passes them through to customers, and CUB says power generators are the ones profiting.

Push for reform

CUB supports the POWER Act in Springfield (SB 4016/HB 5513). Among other provisions, it would require data centers to pay their own interconnection costs and to bring their own new capacity or cut use at peak hours. It would also require them to bring their own new clean power if they want firm service that won't be curtailed during high demand. CUB is also urging PJM to keep data centers that don't bring their own capacity out of the capacity market.

“We must rein in data centers and make sure Big Tech pays its fair share of energy bills,” CUB Communications Director Jim Chilsen said in September.

A partial offset, a CEJA bill credit, is scheduled to end in mid-2027, according to CUB.

Key facts
  • 2026–27 PJM capacity price: $329.17/MW-day (~22% above prior year; ~11x two years earlier)
  • Capacity ≈ 20% of ComEd supply price
  • ComEd price ~53% above 2024
  • Elevated at least through May 2028 (CUB)
  • CUB backs the Illinois POWER Act

Why it matters for your home

Capacity costs are set years in advance by forces no single household controls. Every kilowatt-hour your own panels produce and you use at home is one you don't buy at the utility's supply price. A battery lets you use more of that solar after sunset. CUB notes that net-metering solar customers see the CEJA adjustment in reverse, but says homes with solar should still come out ahead during high-price periods.
